Understanding the Basics

Why Rainwater Harvesting Matters

Understand the science and impact of rooftop rainwater harvesting and artificial groundwater recharge.

What is RTRWH?

Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting (RTRWH) is a technique that collects and stores rainwater falling on rooftops for reuse. It reduces dependence on municipal supply, lowers utility bills, and replenishes local groundwater.

  • Collects runoff from rooftops
  • Filtered and stored in tanks
  • Used for domestic & irrigation needs

Artificial Recharge (AR)

Artificial Recharge directs harvested rainwater into the ground through specially designed structures like recharge pits, trenches, and wells — replenishing depleting aquifers and combating groundwater stress.

  • Recharge pits & trenches
  • Percolation wells
  • Injection well methods

System Recommendations

Tailored recommendations based on standard engineering guidelines.

Recommended Tank Size

Install a storage tank of capacity equal to 20–25 days of water demand. Use food-grade RCC or HDPE tanks with inlet filters and overflow pipes.

Run assessment for personalized sizing.

Recharge Pit Dimensions

Construct recharge pits of 1m × 1m × 2m filled with gravel, broken bricks, and sand layers. Install at least one pit per 50m² of roof area.

Run assessment for personalized sizing.

Recharge Well Suggestion

Where groundwater level is below 10m, use a recharge well of 150–300mm diameter bored 3–5m into the aquifer zone with a pre-filter chamber.

Consult a hydrogeologist for deep aquifer systems.

Maintenance Schedule

Clean first-flush diverters before monsoon season. Inspect and desilt recharge pits annually. Clean storage tanks every 6 months with disinfectant solution.

  • Pre-monsoon: Clean gutters & filters
  • Post-monsoon: Desilting
  • Biannual: Tank cleaning

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about rainwater harvesting and artificial groundwater recharge.

Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting is a system that captures rain falling on rooftops through a network of gutters, downpipes, filters, and storage tanks. The collected water can be used directly for non-potable uses (gardening, flushing, washing) or treated for drinking. It reduces runoff, recharges groundwater, and provides an alternative water source.

The annual harvesting potential is calculated using the formula:
V = R × A × C × η
Where V = Volume (L), R = Annual Rainfall (mm), A = Rooftop Area (m²), C = Runoff Coefficient (0.6–0.9 based on material), η = System efficiency (0.85). A 1mm rainfall on 1m² = 1 litre.

Artificial Recharge (AR) is the intentional process of augmenting natural groundwater recharge by directing harvested or treated water into aquifers through engineered structures. It combats over-extraction, raises water table levels, improves water quality through natural filtration, and can prevent land subsidence in urban areas.

Tank size should meet 20–25 days of water demand. Formula: Tank Capacity = Daily Demand × 20 to 25. For a household of 5 people using 150 L/day each, the total daily demand is 750 L. Recommended tank size = 750 × 20 = 15,000 L. However, this is adjusted based on rainfall patterns, monsoon duration, and available space.

Collected rainwater may contain dust, bird droppings, biological contaminants, and dissolved pollutants. It is NOT recommended for direct drinking without treatment. Standard treatment includes: first-flush diversion (discarding first 1mm of rainfall), multi-stage filtration, UV treatment, and chlorination. Properly treated water can meet IS 10500 potable water standards.

Several national and state government schemes support RWH including: Jal Shakti Abhiyan (National Water Mission), Atal Bhujal Yojana (CGWB), AMRUT 2.0, Tamil Nadu's mandatory RWH requirement, and subsidies provided by various State Ground Water Authorities. Tax rebates may also be available under green building certification programs (GRIHA, LEED).
